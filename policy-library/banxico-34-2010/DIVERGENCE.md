**Five divergences. Two would change an outcome.**

**A sixth was withdrawn.** It read: *the recipient of the dictamen differs between numerals 3.4 and
3.6, and the restatement does not note it.* That is true and it is not a divergence. Numeral 3.6
names the tarjetahabiente; numeral 3.4 names the Titular in one paragraph and the tarjetahabiente in
another, for a document 3.4 specifies by reference to 3.6. **The source is inconsistent with itself
there**, so there is no settled reading for the restatement to have departed from, and what the
restatement does say about numeral 3.4 is accurate. Not recording an open question is not an error
in a document whose stated purpose is the bounds a mandate enforces. The finding is kept, in full,
as ambiguity **A6**, which is where a question the source does not resolve belongs. D1 through D5
keep their numbers.

## D1. Inciso a) is missing its third component (OMISSION, changes outcomes)

**Source, page 13:**

> a) Evidencia de los factores de autenticación utilizados de acuerdo con lo establecido en el
> inciso a) del numeral 2.6, así como la explicación en lenguaje simple y claro de dichos factores
> **y la forma en que su verificación se realizó de acuerdo con los procedimientos aplicables a
> dichos factores de autenticación.**

**Restatement element table:**

> | a) | evidence of the factores de autenticación used, together with an explanation in plain
> language | unconditional |

The source requires **three** things: the evidence, the plain-language explanation of the factors,
and an account of **how their verification was carried out** against the applicable procedures. The
restatement carries two.

**Why it changes outcomes.** A dictamen that evidences the factors and explains them, but says
nothing about how verification was performed, satisfies the restatement and does not satisfy the
source. Every determination made against the restatement's inciso a) is made against a lower bar
than the numeral sets.

## D2. Firmeza depends on which of two periods applies, and the restatement names one (OMISSION, changes outcomes)

**Source, page 14, seventh paragraph:**

> Si transcurrido el plazo a que se refiere el **cuarto o quinto párrafo** de este numeral, **según
> corresponda**, la Emisora no entrega el referido dictamen en los términos señalados, el abono
> realizado previamente por ésta quedará firme, por lo que no podrá revertirse.

**Restatement:**

> **TWO CONDITIONS, AND BOTH ARE REQUIRED.** The abono becomes firme … only where both of the
> following hold: 1. the dictamen period has elapsed, and 2. the Emisora did not deliver the
> dictamen within that period on the terms the numeral states.

The restatement's two conditions are correct. What it omits is that **"the dictamen period" is not
one period**. The source makes firmeza turn on the fourth **or** fifth paragraph deadline *según
corresponda*, and the fifth paragraph's period is 180 días naturales for operations carried out
abroad.

**Why it changes outcomes.** On a foreign operation, a dictamen delivered on day 100 is late under
the restatement's single 45-day reading and timely under the source. The restatement does state the
180-day period, but in a separate section headed "Foreign operations", not connected to the firmeza
test. A reader applying the firmeza section as written reaches the wrong answer on every foreign
case.

## D3. The fifth paragraph carries two rules; the restatement identifies it with one (OMISSION)

**Source, page 14, fifth paragraph** contains both:

1. `En caso de reclamaciones relativas a operaciones realizadas en el extranjero, el plazo previsto
   en el párrafo anterior será de ciento ochenta días naturales.`
2. the Tarjetahabiente's right, on request, to a free copy of the expediente within
   `cuarenta y cinco días naturales` following delivery of the dictamen.

**Restatement:**

> **THIS IS NOT THE OTHER 45-DAY PERIOD IN THE SAME NUMERAL.** The fifth paragraph of numeral 3.6
> provides the Tarjetahabiente a right to a copy of the expediente within "cuarenta y cinco días
> naturales", which is unambiguous.

Correct as far as it goes, and it is a genuinely useful distinction. But it identifies the fifth
paragraph **only** with the expediente right, while placing the 180-day rule under a separate
heading. Since the firmeza paragraph refers to the fifth paragraph by number, the restatement's
own cross-reference does not resolve. This is the mechanism behind D2.

## D4. The clock start is stated more precisely in the source (ADDED PRECISION IN THE SOURCE)

**Source, page 13, fourth paragraph:**

> dentro de un plazo de cuarenta y cinco Días **contado a partir de la fecha en la que se haya
> recibido el aviso a que se refiere el numeral 3.3**

**Restatement:** "The dictamen is due within a period the compiled text states as `cuarenta y cinco
Días`." It does not state what the period is counted from.

The source anchors it to the date the **notice was received**, and numeral 3.3 makes that date a
recorded fact: the Emisora must give the Tarjetahabiente `un número de referencia del aviso, así
como la fecha y hora en que esta se recibió` (page 10). The clock start is therefore an
institutional record, not an inference.

## D5. Delivery channel and signatory are requirements the element table does not carry (OMISSION)

**Source, page 13, fourth paragraph** requires the dictamen to be made available:

- `en alguna sucursal, o bien, a través de los medios que al efecto haya convenido con el
  Tarjetahabiente, **a elección de este último**` — the channel is the holder's choice, and
- `**suscrito por personal de la Emisora facultado para ello**` — signed by personnel authorised to
  do so.

Neither appears in the restatement's element table, which lists only incisos a-d. Both are chapeau
requirements of the same sentence that introduces those incisos, and a dictamen failing either is
not a conforming dictamen for firmeza purposes.

## Where the restatement is right, and materially so

Recorded because a divergence list read alone would misrepresent it.

- **`por lo menos` is a floor.** Source: `que contenga, por lo menos, la información siguiente`.
  The restatement's insistence that a dictamen carrying every listed element is not thereby complete
  is correct and is the kind of thing a naive encoding drops.
- **Inciso d) is conditional and its absence is not a breach.** Source: `En caso de contar con ella`.
  Correct, and correctly load-bearing.
- **Firmeza attaches on expiry, not before, and a deficient dictamen inside the period is curable.**
  Consistent with the source's construction.
- **The Días / Días Hábiles question is unresolved and the restatement refuses to resolve it.**
  Confirmed against the source: the definitions section defines `Días Hábiles` and the fourth
  paragraph says `cuarenta y cinco Días`. Registered as A1.

## Not a divergence from this source

The restatement carries no commencement dates. Neither does this compiled text: it marks 3.6 as
`(Numeral modificado por la Circular 13/2018)` and states no in-force date. The 2019 dates asserted
elsewhere in this estate could not be checked here.
