# EVIDENTIAL: a category the schema did not have

Added to the schema 2026-08-23, on the reg 75 ruling. **Nothing has been recategorised.** The survey
below is the reason.

## The category

**EVIDENTIAL: it governs how a condition is proved, by saying who must prove it and what evidence
does not suffice.**

That sentence mentions neither syntax nor return type, which is E10's test.

**Why the name.** `BURDEN` was rejected because it covers only the allocation half and would leave
reg 75(3), which bounds sufficiency, and 75(4), which requires disclosure, outside a category named
for them. `PROBATIVE` describes evidence rather than the rule about it. `EVIDENTIAL` covers all three
things reg 75 actually does.

**What it is not.** Every clause encoded before this either states a condition or defines a term. A
rule about who must prove a condition is neither: it does not say what must be true, and it does not
say what a word means. It says how the question is answered when the parties disagree.

## Result domain, and what the evaluator does with it

**It has one, and this is the difference from DEFINITIONAL and INSTRUCTION.** A fact does make an
evidential clause true or false: whether the party bearing the burden discharged it. So it is
evaluated rather than refused.

What distinguishes it from MECHANICAL and JUDGMENT is **what its result is about**. A MECHANICAL
clause reports whether a rule was complied with. An EVIDENTIAL clause reports the state of proof of
some other clause's condition, and **its result is an input to that clause, not a determination of
its own**.

That has a consequence the reg 75 ruling makes concrete: `psr-2017/75/1/provider-burden` is not a
conjunct of the refund trigger, and a register that said it was had the relationship backwards.

## The survey, and why nothing was recategorised

The brief set a stop condition: more than one Banxico or FECA clause needing recategorisation is a
finding about the earlier conversions rather than a change to make. **It fires.**

| domain | EVIDENTIAL under the tight test | notes |
|---|---|---|
| **PSR** | **4** | the whole of reg 75, as expected: 75(1) and 75(2) allocate, 75(3) bounds sufficiency, 75(4) requires disclosure |
| **Banxico** | **1** | `34-2010/3.6/p4/floor`. `por lo menos` says the enumerated elements are a minimum and are not sufficient, which is reg 75(3)'s shape exactly, in Spanish |
| **FECA** | **5** | `2/a/occupational-rationale`, `3/d/1/clear-cut`, `3/d/2/rationalized`, `3/d/3/c/emotional`, `4/b/no-opinion` |

**FECA is five, so the recategorisation stops here.**

### And the FECA number is not a surprise once stated

The chapter is titled `Causal Relationship` and most of its content is about **what evidence
establishes it**. `3/d/1/clear-cut` says a diagnosis and an affirmative statement `are sufficient`;
`3/d/2/rationalized` says what is `required` in every other case. Those are sufficiency rules wearing
a condition's clothes, which is exactly what the brief suspected of Banxico's element table.

**Banxico's element table is NOT one, and the distinction is worth keeping.** The incisos say what the
dictamen must CONTAIN, which is a condition on a document. Only the `por lo menos` floor says what
the contents do not suffice to establish. One clause of the five, not the table.

### Two clauses excluded on a stated basis, and it is arguable

`feca/2-0805/3/a/1/countersigned` and `3/a/3/chiropractor` say what `will be considered medical
evidence` and what `constitutes medical evidence only if`. That is **admissibility**: whether a
document enters the record at all, rather than what it proves once there.

**Reg 75 contains no admissibility rule**, so extending the category to cover them would be my
extension rather than one the source drove. They are left MECHANICAL and flagged here, because the
line between admitting evidence and bounding its sufficiency is thinner than this paragraph makes it
sound, and a second domain with an explicit admissibility provision should settle it rather than me.

## What a recategorisation would touch, when it is ruled

PSR 4, Banxico 1, FECA 5. Ten clauses across three domains, of which **six are currently JUDGMENT,
two CONDITIONAL, one MECHANICAL and one DERIVED**. None of the ten would change its result: the
category changes what the register says a clause IS, not what the evaluator computes.
